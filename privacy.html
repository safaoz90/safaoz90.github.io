Aha! Brain Puzzles

Privacy Policy

Last updated: 7 October 2026

Aha! Brain Puzzles (“the game”, “we”) is made by an independent developer. This policy explains what information the iPhone app collects, why, who receives it, and the choices you have. We have tried to keep it short and plain.

In short: no account, no name, no email. Your progress stays on your phone. If you tell the game you are under 13, it sends nothing at all: no statistics and no ads. For players 13 and older, we use anonymous play statistics to make the puzzles better, and rewarded videos you choose to watch pay for the free game.

1. Information kept on your phone

Your stars, stickers, lamps, settings, the age range you choose (for example “18 to 49”), and a record of how each puzzle went (for the “Puzzle stats” screen) are stored only on your phone. We cannot see them. Deleting the app, or Settings → Start over in the game, removes them.

2. The age question

When you first play, the game asks who is playing. This only decides which features are switched on:

  • Under 13: the game sends no statistics, never starts the video ad service, never asks Apple’s tracking question, and shows no video offers. Sharing a puzzle needs a grown-up check first.
  • 13 or older: the services in sections 3 to 5 are used as described.

The game is not directed at children under 13, and it is not in the App Store’s Kids category.

3. Play statistics (Google Analytics for Firebase), players 13 and older

To learn which puzzles are too hard, too easy or not fun, the game sends events such as “puzzle started”, “puzzle solved” (with time taken, mistakes, hints used and stars), “puzzle left”, your “Did you enjoy it?” vote, shop and video use, purchases made, shares, and the age range you chose. Firebase also records basic technical details: an app-install identifier created by Firebase, phone model, iOS and app version, language, approximate country or region (from the network address), and session times.

  • It is switched off until you say you are 13 or older, and switched off again if you change to “Under 13”.
  • It does not use Apple’s advertising identifier (IDFA) or the vendor identifier (IDFV), and is not used for advertising or tracking.
  • It is not linked to your name or Apple Account, which we never receive.
  • Google processes this for us under its Firebase privacy terms. We keep the detailed event data for at most 14 months.

4. Rewarded videos (AppLovin MAX and its ad partners), players 13 and older

You can choose to watch a short video to earn lamps. Videos are provided by AppLovin MAX, which asks these ad networks for a video: Google AdMob, Unity Ads, Liftoff (Vungle) and Meta Audience Network. There are no banners and no videos you did not ask for.

  • The ad service is started only for players 13 and older, and only once videos are first offered (after 5 solved puzzles).
  • When it starts, Apple asks whether to allow tracking. If you allow it, the advertising identifier (IDFA) can be used to show videos that match your interests; if you don’t, videos are not personalised with it. You get the same lamps either way. You can change this at any time in iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.
  • In the EU, the UK and other regions where the law requires it, a consent form (Google’s consent tool) is shown first, and your choices there apply.
  • These companies receive device and network information (such as phone model, iOS version, IP address, language), the advertising identifier if you allowed tracking, and how you interacted with the videos. They use it to show, measure and pay for the videos, to prevent fraud, and, if permitted, to personalise ads. Their own policies apply: AppLovin, Google, Unity, Liftoff and Meta.
  • The game also records how much each watched video earned (an amount, the network and the ad format) in its play statistics.

5. Purchases (Apple and RevenueCat)

Purchases are made through Apple’s App Store. Apple handles payment; we never see your card or Apple Account details. To check what you have bought and to restore purchases, the game uses RevenueCat, which receives an anonymous ID made by the app, your purchase history and receipts from Apple, and basic device details. See RevenueCat’s privacy policy. RevenueCat is started only when you open the shop or restore purchases.

6. Sharing a puzzle

“Challenge a friend” and “Ask someone for help” make a picture of the puzzle and open your phone’s share sheet. You choose who receives it and through which app; we do not receive the picture, the message or who you sent it to.

7. Daily reminder

After a few puzzles the game offers a daily reminder. If you say yes, iPhone asks whether the game may send notifications. Reminders are scheduled on your phone itself: nothing is sent to us or anyone else. Turn them off in the game’s Settings or in iPhone Settings → Notifications.

8. What we don’t do

  • We don’t ask for or collect your name, email address, contacts, photos, location or precise location.
  • We don’t sell personal information. Ad partners may use data for personalised ads only as described in section 4 and as you allow.

9. Your choices and rights

  • Choose “Under 13” or change your age range in Settings → Who’s playing to stop statistics and video ads.
  • Turn off tracking in iPhone Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking, or reset your advertising identifier.
  • Delete the app to remove everything stored on your phone.
  • Depending on where you live (for example the EU, UK or California), you may have the right to ask for access to, correction of, or deletion of personal information, and to object to or limit some uses. Because we don’t know who you are, please include the app version and roughly when you played so we can help. Write to us at the address below; we will answer within 30 days.

10. Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and the date at the top.
